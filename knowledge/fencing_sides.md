# Front and rear roles in fencing

Source: Eloise Newell Clark, “Evaluation of Students in Fencing,” in the NAGWS
Bowling–Fencing Guide (1975–1977), PDF page 92 / printed page 95.
https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED103387.pdf#page=92

This historical teaching guide depicts bent knees in en garde. Its lunge illustration distinguishes
a bent front knee from an extended rear leg. The weapon arm and rear arm also have different roles.
The app should therefore avoid treating unequal left/right knee angles or arm positions as a fault,
or recommending bilateral symmetry as a universal goal.

Application limitation: this is historical instruction, not a modern validated scoring standard.
Identify the front leg, weapon arm and movement phase before discussing side-specific measurements.
2D landmarks alone cannot check the blade, target relationship, spinal curvature or tactical intent.
Use repeatable footage and an instructor’s review when choosing a correction.
