# Gesture recognition: collection and labeling

## Actual starter collection

On 2026-10-04 the raw archive contains **35 unique originals**, approximately
25 minutes 54 seconds. This is **not** the usable modern fencing training duration.
The checksum-bound domain registry approves **30 modern originals / 7 minutes 50 seconds**
and **one 5.7-second modern foil excerpt**. Eight mixed or historical recordings/excerpts
are excluded. Unlisted future downloads default to excluded.

- **Modern drills:** 23 MIT OCW sport fencing videos, CC BY-NC-SA 3.0. Unarmed fencing
  footwork drills are relevant; these are noncommercial development sources.
- **Electric competition:** seven modern Dourdan sabre bout recordings, six CC0 and one
  CC BY-SA 3.0. Resets, walking and breaks are not advance/retreat ground truth.
- **Mixed instruction:** five EVD originals are archived but excluded as complete training
  sources. `evd-esgrima-004` is explicitly historical, including ancient depictions, and
  is excluded from positive and negative training examples. Only the separately inspected
  37.0–42.7 second modern foil excerpt from EVD 001 is eligible for label review.
- **Labels:** 64 assistant draft intervals across 16 clips, including six new footwork drafts.
  All remain provisional. Group footage needs athlete identity and crop review. There are
  **zero approved ground-truth labels**, no verified bounce intervals, and no trained model.
- **Pose preparation:** 12 clips prepared; the modern foil excerpt has 67/69 tracked frames.
  Whole-frame group footage often loses identity or visibility; raw clip counts do not imply
  usable single-athlete training examples.

The source registry is `datasets/source-curation.json`. Approval is bound to the original SHA-256.
`scripts/curate_fencing_sources.py` applies decisions; preparation verifies actual source bytes,
and training checks both the current approval and the prepared video checksum. Source approval
does not approve gesture labels. Provisional labels remain excluded independently.

## Footwork features

`footwork-v2` uses hips, knees and ankles without requiring visible shoulders or wrists.
Its 112 temporal features describe leg-normalized stance width, knee angles, relative foot
motion and optional facing/camera motion channels. Fixed-camera absolute travel is masked
when the camera is moving or unspecified. Directional predictions abstain when facing is unknown.
Missing lower-body landmarks, long gaps and abrupt scale changes reject a window.

On the same four original solo clips, the new feature extractor retains **52/62** candidate
750 ms windows versus **18/62** for the legacy upper-body-dependent extractor. This measures
feature availability, **not classification accuracy**. No model was fitted to these draft labels.
The review UI shows lower-body coverage, stance-width range and unvalidated repeated hip-height
oscillations. These oscillations are not verified bounce events or foot-contact counts.

The audit is generated by `scripts/audit_fencing_data.py`, distinguishing raw originals,
approved modern originals, derived excerpts and excluded material. Media stays ignored under
`data/training/`; Git contains provenance decisions and annotation drafts, not redistributed videos.

## Recognition architecture and collection targets

1. **Track and measure:** keep the pretrained pose extractor. Isolate one athlete for basic
   footwork; for bouts, maintain both athlete identities through pans, cuts and occlusion. Camera
   motion must be separated from athlete motion before interpreting translation as steps.
2. **Footwork model:** temporal pose features for en garde, advance, retreat, lunge, recovery,
   jumps, bouncing and shuffles. The lower-body feature baseline is implemented; a learned
   sequence classifier and reliable multi-athlete tracking remain future work. Short steps need shorter temporal windows.
   The current linear baseline supports 500/750/1000/1500 ms windows; it is not yet trained.
3. **Blade and tactical model:** a separate sequence model using both fencers, distance, weapon,
   blade/hand motion and exchange context. A lunge is not synonymous with an attack. An isolated
   fencer cannot establish counterattack, riposte or right of way. Pretrained RGB video encoders
   may complement pose when fine blade motion is unavailable; this model is future implementation.
4. **Collect a balanced expansion:** target at least 200 reviewed intervals per movement, ten
   distinct athletes, three independent venues/events and varied framing/handedness. These are
   planning targets, not counts already acquired. Include walking, turns, resets, occlusion and
   unknown actions as negatives. Target dedicated flèche footage and confirmed counterattack
   exchanges next, rather than inventing missing classes from sabre footage.
5. **Review:** assistant/heuristic proposals stay provisional. A human checks actor identity and
   timestamps. Tactical labels require a coach and visible opponent context; doubtful calls remain
   unknown. Double-review a subset and record disagreement/boundary differences. Movement labels
   describe what occurred, not whether technique was good or bad.
6. **Train and evaluate:** keep every source match/course, crop, adjacent interval, re-encoding and
   augmentation together. Prefer athlete/event-disjoint evaluation and an untouched final holdout.
   Report per-class precision/recall, macro F1, event-boundary error and abstention coverage. Tune on
   validation footage before final holdout evaluation. The existing single-split F1 promotion gate
   is only a development gate, not production readiness or independent proof of generalization.
7. **Coaching integration:** feed measured joints, reviewed/detected movement events and uncertainty
   into the LLM. The LLM explains evidence and suggests practice; its guesses do not become labels
   or create universal technique thresholds.

## Local workflow

Open **http://127.0.0.1:8000/dataset** for the source library, slow playback, timestamp seeks,
athlete/crop context, provisional/confirmed labels and JSON export. Saves keep revision history.
This local research workspace is separate from the athlete's practice minutes and daily logs.

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/collect_fencing_data.py
# Refresh source metadata without downloading additional Wikimedia media:
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/collect_commons_fencing.py --inventory-only
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/curate_fencing_sources.py
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/seed_fencing_labels.py
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/seed_fencing_labels.py --labels datasets/footwork-expansion-annotations.json
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/prepare_fencing_data.py --clips step_forward step_back jumpe_lunge jump_forward_jump_back
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/audit_fencing_data.py
# After actual human review and sufficient independent data:
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts/train_gestures.py --data data/training --window-ms 750 --output models/gestures.json
```

Re-run preparation after changing a crop or label review. Approved footwork labels supported by the
current baseline are exported to `prepared/annotations.json`; provisional labels remain only in
the review records. Bounce/jump/shuffle labels are supported by the feature baseline; flèche and tactical labels stay in the richer schema for later models. User-upload training labels also require explicit modern fencing domain confirmation.
Training refuses insufficient reviewed examples/groups and never silently lowers the data gate.

Dataset saves and revision locks are local filesystem operations. This research workspace is not
yet a distributed annotation platform or an authenticated public endpoint.
